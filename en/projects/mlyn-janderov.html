A new website for a family flour mill that has been milling since 1540. It brought 258 clicks from Google in the first two months.

The client

A family business whose history goes back to 1540. It still mills flour and sells animal feed. They want to deal with flour, not websites.

Where it started

An outdated site nobody in the company could find their way around. What the client asked for, word for word, was “a completely simple site”. History, prices, contact details. And above all, nothing for them to deal with.

Before and after

Before After

Drag to compare the old site with the new one.

How it was built

The material arrived the way a family business keeps it. Four photos, a price list and the copy in office documents. Three rounds of design were enough, because the client wasn’t commenting on a written description but on a live preview at a temporary address. He wrote his comments about what he could actually see. Photos sent at midday were on the site by the evening.

The hard part wasn’t the design, it was rescuing the technical side. The only records of the old site were on paper, with the password crossed out and rewritten illegibly. The one the client sent me didn’t work. So I asked the original provider to restore access, sorted out the authorisation for the domain transfer (the client couldn’t open the attachment his registrar had sent, so I dealt with that for him too) and put the site on new hosting.

In all that time the client didn’t have to touch anything technical. That was exactly the brief. The site runs on its own domain today and I still look after the hosting and the upkeep.
