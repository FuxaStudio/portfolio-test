Nobody had to ring round the guests

A site for wedding guests, in the design of the printed invitation. 30 completed forms came in for 51 guests, one form per family, and out of them came the meal counts, the drinks and the allergies.

Client
A couple from Košice, Slovakia
Field
A wedding for 51 guests
Scope
Site, form, a summary sheet and a printed seating plan
Demo
demo version ↗

The client

A couple getting married in Košice, in eastern Slovakia. The job came through the groom’s sister, and she was the one I dealt with on the copy and the comments throughout. The site was only ever meant to live until the wedding day, which is exactly why it had to work.

Where it started

In the Czech Republic and Slovakia weddings still go out as a printed invitation. The guests had one in their hands, and with it a pile of questions that wouldn’t fit on the card. Attendance, meal choice, allergies and the welcome toast were all being collected by hand. The brief was to tune the site to the design of the invitation and put a form in it that one person could fill in for a whole family.

How it was built

The design didn’t start from a blank page. The printed invitation and a photo of the decorated hall were the reference, so the palette and the typefaces were fixed in advance. The couple saw the first version two days after the brief, and from the start they commented on the live site. It went through two rounds that settled the copy, the order of the sections and small things like a smiley that had to be the typed kind, not an emoji.

The form was the most work. One person confirms a whole family in it, the children’s section only appears if someone is bringing children, and nothing is pre-filled, so it can’t be clicked through blindly. The answers land in a sheet that counts the portions per menu choice by itself, along with the alcoholic and non-alcoholic welcome toasts and the list of allergies. The couple could see it from day one, so they never had to ask anyone for numbers.

The rest was everything that wouldn’t fit on the invitation. A map with the parking marked, both at the ceremony and at the reception. A photo gallery guests upload to without creating an account and without being able to delete anyone else’s pictures. A password on the entrance, because the site was on a public domain. And a QR code for the tables. The couple kept adding to the running order as they went, which is why it’s deliberately open-ended and the line after the last item fades into nothing.

Key decisions

A hand-coded site

Palette and typefaces taken from the printed invitation.

One form per family

Nothing pre-filled. The children’s section only when there are children.

A sheet that counts itself

Portions, toasts and allergies without retyping anything.

How it ran

11 days from brief to launch

  1. Day 0
    The brief by email. A document with the copy, the invitation and a photo of the decorated hall.
  2. Day 2
    The first version online. The couple commented straight on the live site.
  3. Day 10
    After two rounds of changes the form writes straight into the summary sheet.
  4. Day 11
    Password, photo gallery and the QR code for the tables. The site finished, six weeks before the wedding.

The screenshots and the link go to a demo version. The names, the date, the places and the menu in it are invented, for the privacy of the couple and their guests.

The same names made the seating plan

The job also included an A1 board that guests looked for as they came into the hall. It was set in the same typography as the site and the invitation, so the printed and the digital half of the wedding looked like one thing.

The names came straight out of the form. The couple retyped nothing, they just split the confirmed guests across ten tables.

What came out of the form

The answers didn’t arrive as a pile. Every form was broken out into one row per person, and a second sheet counted up what they needed to read out to the restaurant as it came in. Meal counts, toasts, allergies and what was needed for the children.

An example of how the sheet is structured. The names, contact details and guest messages are invented, the totals are real.
